## 1. Outcome and concept reconciliation
Can do: express a want, a need, an obligation, a planned action, and ability or inability; distinguish them in a short unfamiliar spoken situation; change the intention without changing the action.

Preserve these canonical IDs:
C001 quiero... — want. Master coverage S1U1C1 noun use and S1U1C2 infinitive use. Example: Quiero comer. Both authoring targets refer to senses/uses of the existing concept, not two replacement IDs.
C002 necesito... — need. Master coverage S1U1C3 noun use and S1U1C4 infinitive use. Examples: Necesito ayuda. Necesito dormir.
C004 tengo que... — obligation. Master coverage S1U2C2. Example: Tengo que esperar.
C006 voy a... — planned action with an infinitive. Master coverage S1U3C2. Example: Voy a salir.
C007 puedo... — ability or permission depending on context. This Arc assesses practical ability/availability, not permission. Master coverage S1U4C1. Example: Puedo ayudar.
C008 no puedo... — inability or unavailable action. Negative variant within S1U4C1; do not invent a new master target to force a one-to-one crosswalk. Example: No puedo salir ahora.
C090 infinitive chaining — construction support across S1U1C2, S1U1C4, S1U2C2, S1U3C2 and S1U4C1. Evidence is scoped to these frames, not every infinitive construction.
C089 high-frequency irregular present forms — notice/use quiero, tengo, voy and puedo within familiar frames only. No full paradigm or second-person gate in Arc 1. Other forms remain for later Arcs.

Supporting/recycled canonical concepts:
C057 ir; C059 hacer; C075 comer; C077 dormir; C086 salir (existing Arc map).
C081 esperar; C082 ayudar; C068 hablar (small lexical bridge for this pack).
C044 ahora; C045 después; C047 hoy; C048 mañana; C040 sí/no; C041 por favor; C042 gracias (supported scene language, not Arc 1 unlock requirements).
C003 tengo for the contrast with C004; C091 negation and C099 connected-speech chunking receive narrow observations only.
Do not automatically award C057, C060, C061 or C062 lemma mastery from a successful voy/tengo/quiero/puedo response. Construction and lemma evidence are not interchangeable.

Entry support: explain the scene in English; supply comer, salir, esperar, ayudar and dormir through audio plus optional meaning. A beginner can replay an approximately 60-second phrase preview. Preview is exposure. Repair controls provide Replay, Slower version and meaning-on-demand; their use is logged rather than penalized as failure.
Lexical bridge: ayuda = help; tiempo = time; música = music; agua = water. Record lexicon entries without manufacturing C101+ IDs. Extra scene words remain supported/exposure-only until separately taught.

## 3. Song — Ahora sí
Concept: a night-out track about wanting to go, having to wait, then actually making a plan. Glossy, bass-led dance/rap-pop with a playful female lead; general audience, clean lyrics, not a personal biography.
Pedagogical job: an earworm for three intention frames. Supporting words are not secretly tested. Translation lives in optional support, not an English explanation after every line.

Lyrics — candidate for performance and native-speaker review:

Producer prompt — paste into the music-style field:
Glossy bilingual dance-pop with rap-pop attitude, 104–110 BPM, punchy syncopated drums, warm sub-bass, crisp claps, tight percussion, neon synth stabs and a little late-night guitar texture. Playful confident female lead, intimate verses, melodic chant-hook chorus, tasteful spoken-sung pickups and short ad-libs. Establish the hook within ten seconds; keep the Spanish frames quiero salir, tengo que esperar and voy a salir clearly intelligible with natural word stress. Give each phrase breathing room; no rapid tongue-twister delivery. Compact verses, rising pre-chorus, bass-forward chorus, stripped bridge and a final energetic lift. Clean lyrics, contemporary radio-ready mix, bright but not childish. Avoid classroom chanting, nursery-song melodies, dramatic motivational narration, oversized choir, heavy vocal distortion and long instrumental intros.

Language notes: bailar is an additional supported lexicon entry, not an unlock target. “Tengo que — got to” is a hook gloss, not a complete grammar definition. “Voy a” is glossed as going to only when it introduces an action. Do not use a destination example to prove future-intention comprehension.
Alternate treatments: (1) indie dance with live bass/guitar and an understated mixed-gender vocal; (2) atmospheric melodic R&B with half-time drums and a shorter hook. Preserve approved Spanish wording and concept coverage. Genre preference must not change the gate.
Version family: original supported mix; later Spanish-heavy rewrite with a separate lyric map; instrumental/recall cut only after the original works. Do not fabricate a Spanish percentage: measure intelligible lyric tokens on the final rendition.
Listening test: does the hook come back later, and can the learner use tengo que esperar in plain speech without the tune? The latter needs an actual response check.

## 4. Spoken episode — El plan cambió
Cast: Sofía, decisive but flexible; Mateo, friendly and easily distracted; narrator, warm and brief. No caricatured accent or exaggerated slow pronunciation. Use ordinary conversational recordings plus a separately recorded clear version. First reveal scene stakes in English, then let Spanish carry the exchange.
A scene-only edit removes narrator questions and silent response gaps. The active edit uses the exact segments below. Bracketed directions are not spoken. A model answer is revealed only after a response/skip. English support can be omitted for learners with demonstrated comprehension.

Chapter A — The door and the plan

A01 Narrator: Sofía wants to go out. Mateo needs a little time. Listen for the difference between wanting, having to do something, and a plan.
A02 Sofía: Quiero salir.
A03 Mateo: Tengo que esperar.
A04 Sofía: Está bien. Voy a esperar.
A05 [Active prompt] Narrator: Who wants to go out: Sofía or Mateo? [Pause five seconds; tap or optional voice.]
A06 Narrator: Sofía. Quiero salir means I want to go out. Mateo says Tengo que esperar: I have to wait.
A07 Sofía: Quiero comer.
A08 Mateo: Voy a comer después.
A09 Narrator: He plans to eat later. He didn’t say he has to eat.
A10 [Active recall] Narrator: Tell Mateo you want to eat. [Pause seven seconds.]
A11 Narrator: One way: Quiero comer.
A12 Mateo: Tengo tiempo. Pero tengo que esperar.
A13 Narrator: Tengo tiempo: I have time. Tengo que esperar: I have to wait. That que changes the job of the phrase.
A14 [Active manipulation] Narrator: Keep comer. Change Quiero comer into a plan: I’m going to eat. [Pause seven seconds.]
A15 Narrator: Voy a comer.
A16 Sofía: Voy a salir después.
A17 Mateo: Sí. Voy a esperar ahora.
A18 [Active open response] Narrator: Choose a real or fictional action. Say something you want to do, then something you plan to do. You can use the same action, but make the two intentions clear. [Pause twelve seconds.]
A19 Narrator: For example: Quiero salir. Voy a salir después. Your answer can be different.
A20 [Scene reprise without narrator] Sofía: Quiero salir. Mateo: Tengo que esperar. Sofía: Está bien. Voy a esperar.
A21 Narrator: Next: what you need, what you can do, and what you can’t do right now.

Chapter B — A workable plan

B01 Narrator: Same afternoon. Sofía needs help. Mateo can help, but can’t go out yet. Ayuda means help; ayudar means to help.
B02 Sofía: Necesito ayuda.
B03 Mateo: Puedo ayudar.
B04 Sofía: Gracias. Quiero salir ahora.
B05 Mateo: No puedo salir ahora. Tengo que esperar.
B06 [Active listening] Narrator: Is Mateo saying he doesn’t want to go out, or that he can’t go out now? [Pause five seconds.]
B07 Narrator: He can’t go out now. No puedo does not mean I don’t want to.
B08 Sofía: Está bien. Voy a esperar.
B09 Mateo: Necesito comer.
B10 Sofía: Puedo esperar.
B11 [Active recall] Narrator: You need to sleep. Say it. [Pause seven seconds.]
B12 Narrator: Necesito dormir.
B13 [Active manipulation] Narrator: Puedo salir. Keep salir, but say you cannot go out. [Pause seven seconds.]
B14 Narrator: No puedo salir.
B15 [Active scene reply] Sofía: Necesito ayuda.
B16 Narrator: You have time and are able to help. Answer Sofía in Spanish. [Pause eight seconds.]
B17 Narrator: One answer: Puedo ayudar.
B18 [Optional transfer practice] Mateo: Voy a salir ahora.
B19 Narrator: Your situation changed: you can’t leave now because you have to wait. Tell Mateo both things. [Pause twelve seconds.]
B20 Narrator: For example: No puedo salir ahora. Tengo que esperar.
B21 Sofía: Voy a esperar. Después, quiero comer.
B22 Mateo: Sí. Puedo salir después.
B23 Narrator: A want, a need, an obligation, a plan, and what’s possible. Keep listening if you like; a Quick Check can see what you can use without these examples.

Production direction: let the scene breathe, but do not pad pauses when the learner has already responded. Scene-only version is entertainment/exposure, not assessed production. If no recording/evaluation was captured, log optional spoken-along work as practice only. A response after A11 or B12 has been revealed is supported rehearsal, not uncued retrieval.
Audio QC: do not merge the sung and spoken pronunciation models. Confirm intelligible tengo que and voy a at normal conversational timing with different speakers.

## 5. Games — actual item banks
Flip It rule: change the meaning frame, preserve the action, no word bank by default. Spanish source text is allowed here because the evidence is manipulation, not listening. Display prompt language and support mode honestly.
F01 Quiero comer → make it a plan. Key: Voy a comer. Concepts C001/C006/C090.
F02 Voy a salir → make it an obligation. Key: Tengo que salir. C006/C004/C090.
F03 Tengo que esperar → express a want instead. Key: Quiero esperar. C004/C001/C090.
F04 Quiero dormir → express a need. Key: Necesito dormir. C001/C002/C090.
F05 Puedo ayudar → make it negative. Key: No puedo ayudar. C007/C008; narrow C091 observation.
F06 No puedo salir → make it positive. Key: Puedo salir. C008/C007.
F07 Necesito comer → express ability instead. Key: Puedo comer. C002/C007/C090.
F08 Puedo esperar → make it a plan. Key: Voy a esperar. C007/C006/C090.
Hints: meaning reminder → partial frame → full answer. Track every hint and reveal. Repeated exact items can help practice but cannot supply fresh readiness evidence. Accept yo-prefixed forms and small punctuation/case variation. Reject invented frames such as quiero como, tengo salir, or voy salir for the relevant construction. Do not reject a natural paraphrase as a communication failure; record whether the intended construction was actually demonstrated.
No speed pressure for beginners. Optional timed play later cannot turn slow accurate output into a knowledge failure.

Scene Rescue R01: Your friend needs help; you are available. Audio: Necesito ayuda. Open prompt: answer usefully. Candidate: Puedo ayudar. Successful alternatives may accomplish the task, but only score C007 when ability language is actually demonstrated.
R02: You are invited out now, but you must wait. Open prompt: explain the constraint. Candidate: No puedo salir ahora. Tengo que esperar. Score task success and C008/C004 separately.
R03: You want to eat, but only plan to eat later. Open prompt: tell your friend both things. Candidate: Quiero comer. Voy a comer después. C001/C006.
Optional choices for R02: No puedo salir ahora / Quiero salir ahora / Voy a salir ahora. Choosing the first yields recognition in context; it does not prove spoken production.
Hook Hunt excerpt: play the approved chorus up to “Tengo que…”; ask for its missing continuation. Expected esperar. This is lyric-cued recall of the continuation, not independent obligation production.
Finish the Bar can reuse that excerpt as entertainment. Do not double-count the same response across two mechanics.

## 6. Speaking mission — Today, but with a twist
Warm-up, unscored unless captured: say one thing you want, one thing you need, and one thing you plan to do. Fictional answers are welcome.
Scenario: arrange a simple afternoon with a friend. Known actions are comer, salir, esperar and ayudar. Tell them a preference, an obligation and a plan. Then the friend changes timing.
Interlocutor line: Voy a salir ahora.
Constraint shown in English or illustrated: you cannot leave now; you have to wait.
Response opportunity: explain that constraint, then offer something you can do instead.
Candidate: No puedo salir ahora. Tengo que esperar. Puedo ayudar.
Optional follow-up: tell the friend a need, such as sleep or food. Do not supply the Spanish frame before the learner answers.
This is a task with explicit targets/constraints, so evidence is independent target production when uncued—not automatically spontaneous transfer. A genuinely unannounced use in a later different interaction can become a transfer candidate.
Success rubric 0–3 for task completion, meaning comprehension, target form and intelligibility: 0 not demonstrated; 1 substantial support; 2 succeeds with minor/recoverable problems; 3 independent and appropriate. Keep target-form scores separate from the overall conversation.
Pronunciation: intelligibility matters; no accent/perfect-trill gate. Text transcription alone does not prove pronunciation accuracy.

## 7. Quick Check — candidate bank A
The learner can attempt this before instruction. Do not play the teaching episode or show the song immediately beforehand as a mandatory warm-up. Say: “Let’s see what’s already useful. We’ll only work on the pieces that need it.”
Record speaker, asset/version, prompt novelty, response, support, evaluation uncertainty and technical status.

Listening block, no transcript before response:
L01 Audio Quiero comer. Ask: what does the speaker want to do? Key eat; C001.
L02 Audio Necesito dormir. Ask: is this a need, a plan or an ability? Key need; C002. Choice-supported listening.
L03 Audio Tengo que salir. Ask for the meaning without choices. Key have to leave/go out; C004.
L04 Audio Voy a ayudar. Ask for the meaning without choices. Key going to help/intends to help; C006.
L05 Audio Puedo esperar. Ask: can this person wait? Key yes; C007.
L06 Audio No puedo salir. Ask: can this person leave? Key no; C008.
Do not accept “wants to” for tengo que or “doesn’t want to” for no puedo. Each question proves only its sampled function. Vary item order and speaker; no repeating yes/no answer pattern.
L02 choice support is explicitly lower-strength; if no other C002 listening evidence exists, follow with a fresh no-choice item Necesito ayuda and ask what they need.
If a clip fails, skip its learning score; offer retry or a clearly labeled text-mode practice equivalent. Text mode does not satisfy listening readiness.

Manipulation:
M01 Tengo que comer → change it to a plan. Key Voy a comer.
M02 Puedo ayudar → make it negative. Key No puedo ayudar.
M03 Quiero salir → express a need instead. Key Necesito salir.
These sample C090 and narrow negation/known-form flexibility. A non-Spanish meaning cue is allowed, but a partial Spanish frame is support.

Uncued production, no Spanish word bank or frame:
P01 You want to sleep. Say it. Candidate Quiero dormir.
P02 You need help. Say it. Candidate Necesito ayuda.
P03 You must wait before leaving. Say the obligation. Candidate Tengo que esperar.
P04 You plan to eat later. Say the plan. Candidate Voy a comer después.
P05 You are able to help. Offer that. Candidate Puedo ayudar.
P06 You cannot go out now. Explain that. Candidate No puedo salir ahora.
These are elicited retrieval/production, not spontaneous speech. Accept meaningful variations and other known lexical substitutions consistent with the situation. If a paraphrase avoids the target, mark task success but target not observed; use a fresh follow-up to sample the missing frame.

Practical close: a new character asks in English-supported context to make a plan. Learner expresses a preference, responds to a timing constraint, and proposes an action. Change roles and actions from the teaching scene. Record communication and repair; do not mechanically require every frame in one answer.

Readiness proposal, explicitly provisional:
Each of six communicative frames needs at least one valid transcript-free listening success and one uncued meaning-appropriate generated use, either from this check or recent eligible evidence. A choice-only success needs a changed no-choice follow-up before being treated as secure listening.
C090 needs correct finite-frame + infinitive structure in at least three distinct relevant constructions across responses. M01–M03 need meaningful changed-form success; preserve earlier passed evidence and recheck only a weak operation. C089 is observed only for the actual first-person frames used, never marked comprehensively mastered.
For an uncertain answer, collect a second fresh example rather than making the first ambiguity a failure. Hinted/revealed answers are practice. Keep willingness to record separate from ability; a written route can demonstrate productive language, but label spoken production unobserved.
Unlock Arc 2 provisionally when these narrow readiness conditions are met. Retention is pending until delayed fresh checks; do not claim retained mastery from one session. No 8/10 total, album completion condition, or global fluency certificate.
If other dimensions are strong but one is weak, preserve successes and recommend a Booster. Exact policies must be calibrated against human review in the pilot.

## 8. Fresh retest and delayed bank B
Fresh means a new utterance/action/speaker or meaningful context variation—not a reskin of the revealed answer. Keep retest assets separate from teaching assets.
Listening candidates: Quiero ayudar; Necesito comer; Tengo que dormir; Voy a esperar; Puedo hablar; No puedo ayudar. Ensure every action is known before scoring. Ask open meanings; avoid showing the transcript.
Production candidates: want to help; need to eat; must leave; plan to sleep; can wait; cannot help. Do not show the corresponding Spanish model until after response.
Manipulation candidates: Quiero ayudar → Tengo que ayudar; Voy a esperar → Puedo esperar; No puedo hablar → Puedo hablar.
Delayed contexts: coordinate a meal instead of an outing; respond to a friend’s voice note; arrange help instead of personal plans. Use a different actor and ordinary speech.
Schedule eligible fresh checks around the following day and about a week later, configurable to performance. Ask a few due items, not all six every time. Delay intervals are starting defaults, not experimentally guaranteed optimal values.
Never certify the broad C089/C090 concepts solely from this narrow bank.

## 9. Two-minute Boosters
Booster 01 — want versus plan (C001/C006).
0–25 sec: plain audio Quiero salir / Voy a salir; explain want versus intended action in one sentence.
25–55: new listening contrast Quiero comer / Voy a comer. Ask which is a plan.
55–90: change Quiero ayudar into a plan; then generate one own want and one plan.
90–120: fresh ordinary-speech item from an unused retest pool; no melody or visible frame. If unsuccessful, stop the check and offer support rather than cycling indefinitely.

Booster 02 — tener versus tener que (C003/C004; C090 narrow).
0–25: Tengo tiempo / Tengo que esperar. Highlight possession versus obligation.
25–55: hear Tengo agua / Tengo que comer and explain the difference.
55–90: say you have to leave; vary the action with one known verb.
90–120: fresh obligation retrieval. Do not require possession mastery to gate the whole Arc.
Optional not-required clarification: No tengo que salir = I don’t have to go out, not I must not go out. It can be explained when needed, but is not an added first-release unlock target.

Booster 03 — can versus cannot (C007/C008; C091 narrow).
0–25: Puedo ayudar / No puedo ayudar.
25–55: new voice Puedo esperar / No puedo esperar; identify which person can wait.
55–90: flip one familiar affirmative and one negative response.
90–120: independently explain a new availability constraint. No preference/inability conflation.

Booster 04 — second verb stays in the infinitive (C090).
0–25: model Quiero comer / Voy a comer / Tengo que comer.
25–55: brief contrast with erroneous quiero como; say that the action stays comer after these frames.
55–90: change the frame while preserving dormir, then ayudar.
90–120: fresh uncued response using a known action. Score only the structure actually produced.
For a hearing/technical problem, do not automatically prescribe grammar practice. For lexical failure, teach the missing action first. For anxiety or repeated difficulty, offer a pause or another surface.
